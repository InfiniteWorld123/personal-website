# Content decisions

This is the decision register for client-facing content, messaging, and page
composition. It exists so future agents do not reconstruct business decisions
from chat history or confuse the currently implemented copy with the approved
next direction.

## Source of truth

- `project.md` owns product scope and business boundaries.
- `positioning.md` owns the strategic message and page responsibilities.
- This file owns accepted content decisions and their practical defaults.
- `src/frontend/content/` is the current implementation, not proof that every
  future content decision has already shipped.

Status: **Accepted** unless marked otherwise. A default guides the public copy;
it is not a rule that rejects an otherwise suitable client.

---

### C1 — Local first, remotely open

The public message starts from Erfurt and Germany because that is the real,
checkable base. Suitable remote clients remain welcome from anywhere when they
can work in German, English, or Arabic.

**Copy rule:** do not lead with “worldwide” or imply that clients outside
Germany are excluded. Say where Yaman is based, then make remote collaboration
clear.

---

### C2 — A direct, human technical partner

Yaman is a self-employed individual, not an agency and not a distant vendor.
The default voice is direct, friendly, and professional: the client is heard,
the technical work is owned by Yaman, and important direction changes are
reviewed together.

**Copy rule:** use first-person singular. Warmth is the default, while the
actual client relationship can become more formal when the client prefers it.
Do not frame friendliness as informality or reduced rigour.

---

### C3 — The work Yaman wants to be known for

The aspirational flagship is a reliable business system tailored to one
company’s real workflow: practical, understandable, maintainable, and useful
for years. It should remove administrative friction rather than demonstrate
technology for its own sake.

There is no default system user. Depending on the company, the system may be
for the owner, employees, customers, or a combination. User roles and features
are discovered from the client’s real workflow; public copy must never imply a
fixed product template.

Websites and online shops remain first-class services. The flagship direction
does not refuse smaller or simpler projects; it explains the long-term depth
Yaman wants his body of work to demonstrate.

**Copy rule:** describe controllable outcomes such as clearer information,
usable workflows, reliable operation, and easier administration. Never claim
guaranteed sales, conversion, rankings, revenue, or savings.

---

### C4 — A rough idea is enough to begin

A prospect does not need a technical specification or a finished plan before
contacting Yaman. They need to be able to describe the goal, problem, or next
step in their own words.

**Copy rule:** reduce the barrier to contact without promising that every idea
will be built. The first conversation clarifies fit and the realistic next
step.

---

### C5 — Landing-page purpose and fixed design boundary

The landing page’s job is to help a cold visitor understand the offer, see
honest proof, and start a conversation. Its existing visual design, layout,
animation, typography, colours, portrait, and interaction treatment are
accepted and must not be redesigned during the content pass.

**Copy rule:** change only content, content visibility, and section inclusion.
Do not use a content task as a reason to alter visual identity or motion.

---

### C6 — Lean landing-page sequence

The approved home-page sequence is:

1. Hero
2. Services
3. Work
4. How it works
5. Final CTA

The home-page story illustration, fit/not-fit section, and brief about teaser
do not belong in this pass. Their information may remain available on the
relevant dedicated pages or be reconsidered later; this is not a deletion of
the underlying content.

**Why:** the visitor needs a clear offer, proof, and a safe next step before
they need extra explanation or qualification.

---

### C7 — Hero content direction

Keep the existing hero visual treatment, greeting, and animated
`WEB / SHOP / SOFTWARE — ENTWICKLER` line. The client-facing message around it
uses this direction:

- small identity: self-employed developer from Erfurt;
- core promise: a direct partner for digital projects;
- supporting promise: listen, plan clearly, and build something the client can
  genuinely use;
- remote badge: `In Erfurt zu Hause · remote für dich`;
- language invitation beside direct email: German, English, or Arabic;
- reassurance near the primary action: a finished plan is not required to
  begin;
- secondary action: show what Yaman has built;
- two short visitor situations: developing a business, or bringing order to a
  workflow.

The exact German master copy is written as one coherent editorial pass, then
adapted naturally to English and Arabic. It must not be translated word for
word when that would sound unnatural.

---

### C8 — Service-card hierarchy on the home page

Keep the order: **Websites → Online-Shops → Individuelle Software**. Do not
show prices on the home page and do not lead the shop card with Shopify.

- **Websites:** a clear home for the business’s information and next steps.
- **Online-Shops:** a store ready to launch and hand over for the owner to run.
- **Individuelle Software:** a tool shaped around the company’s own way of
  working, not a generic system imposed on it.

Detailed scope, pricing, Shopify, and technical examples belong on
`/services`, not in the home-page cards.

---

### C9 — Honest proof and ongoing contact

The home page presents the current projects with equal treatment and labels
them honestly as own work, never customer work. No project receives a premium
or “featured” claim merely to manufacture authority.

The process section, not the hero, explains that Yaman remains the client’s
contact when the project continues after launch. This signals a possible
long-term relationship without promising indefinite or free support.

---

### C10 — Developer first; technical partner in the relationship

Yaman’s professional identity is a self-employed full-stack developer who
builds websites, online shops, web applications, and tailored business systems.
“Technical partner” describes the client relationship: Yaman takes ownership
of the technical side and works from the client’s real needs. It does not
replace or soften the fact that he is a developer.

**Copy rule:** in German, use the natural client-facing description
`selbstständiger Web- und Softwareentwickler`, not a vague consultant label or
an employer-facing “Full-Stack Developer” headline. Design ability supports a
good digital product; it is not a claim that Yaman is a design studio. Marketing
knowledge may inform practical choices, but Yaman is not a marketing agency.

---

### C11 — What Yaman promises

Yaman promises a system that works as agreed, fits the understood needs of the
client, and is built with accountable technical care. The agreed deliverable
must be usable, reflect the client’s brand where that is in scope, and defects
within the agreed post-launch period must be corrected.

He will make responsible implementation choices that support discoverability,
clarity, performance, accessibility, and a sound user journey where they are
part of the project. These are engineering efforts, not outcome guarantees.

**Never promise:** revenue, sales, conversion rate, ranking position, traffic,
or a financial result. Marketing or SEO insight can be shared honestly as
context, never sold as a guaranteed-growth service.

---

### C12 — Why a client chooses Yaman

The client is not pushed through a one-size-fits-all sales funnel. Yaman listens
to the business, asks what matters, defines a fair scope, and builds the right
level of solution for that situation. Reusable foundations and components are
allowed when they serve the client; presenting a generic template as a tailored
solution is not.

**Copy rule:** communicate attentive partnership, clear scope, and fair value.
Do not use defensive language about being cheaper, “not greedy”, or better than
agencies. Let the practical way of working demonstrate that distinction.

---

### C13 — Trust is mutual and adaptable

The desired client experience is mutual trust: the client receives what was
understood and promised, and Yaman receives fair compensation for responsible
work. The default tone is warm and direct, never cold or bureaucratic. It adapts
to a client who prefers a more formal relationship without becoming impersonal.

**Copy rule:** invite conversation, explain the next step plainly, and treat
the visitor as a person rather than a lead number. Do not make exaggerated
claims of friendship, availability, or intimacy.

---

### C14 — Services page: one honest guide, not a catalogue contract

`/services` remains one page containing the three offers in this order:
**Websites → Online-Shops → Individuelle Software**. It helps a prospective
client recognise the right starting point and price band; it is not a fixed
package catalogue or a substitute for the written proposal.

Show honest starting prices. `ab` means the smallest clear, worthwhile scope
for that service, not a final binding quote. The final price follows a first
conversation and a review of the actual scope. Do not lower a price merely to
compete with low-cost template work, and do not use defensive comparisons with
other providers.

Each service explains its typical result, who it helps, what it may include,
and its starting price. Public copy stays positive and specific; it does not
repeat a defensive “what I do not promise” panel under every service. Exact
quantities, client-specific requirements, legal details, content
responsibilities, and delivery terms belong in the written offer, not in public
page copy.

**Honesty rule:** do not manufacture sales, ranking, revenue, or legal claims
in the first place. The page need not repeatedly deny promises it never makes.
Detailed boundaries are explained honestly when they matter to a real request
or in a later FAQ, rather than turning the services page into a contract.

- **Websites:** explain a business and make a clear next step easy. A simple
  contact form, booking handoff, or content integration can still belong here;
  a business-critical workflow does not.
- **Online-Shops:** publicly use the general term `Online-Shop`. Shopify is
  named inside the detail only as the usual option when it fits, never as a
  claim that Yaman is Shopify-only.
- **Individuelle Software:** lead with a business process that needs a
  tailored, reliable tool. A founder's first real product or SaaS can be a
  secondary example, not the public page's main identity.

The page does not sell a standalone paid discovery service yet. An initial
conversation is free and bounded; detailed planning begins after a project is
commissioned. For a complex request, public copy says that the right first step
is clarified before a final offer, without promising a free workshop, detailed
concept, or instant quote.

**Technical copy rule:** do not lead service buyers with a technology stack.
Name a technology only where it explains a practical decision (for example,
Shopify for a suitable shop). Architecture and stack detail live on `/stack`.

**After launch:** continuation is possible for operations, maintenance, or a
next improvement under a separate agreement. Do not imply free, indefinite
support or a mandatory retainer.

---

### C15 — About page: professional person, not private biography

`/about` builds trust in Yaman as the person a client will work with. It is not
a life story, a CV, or a request for sympathy. Lead with the clear professional
identity `selbstständiger Web- und Softwareentwickler aus Erfurt`; immediately
name what he builds: websites, online shops, and individual web applications.

`Dein technischer Partner` describes the working relationship, not the job
title. It may appear after the professional identity to explain that Yaman
takes responsibility for the technical side of a project. It must never stand
alone as an ambiguous label for general IT, hardware, or cloud support.

The existing page composition remains: introduction and portrait, three short
chapters, working principles, what collaboration means, platform note, and
CTA. Change content only. The chapters explain professional thinking — useful
technology, systems that hold together, and clarity before implementation —
instead of migration, family, language-learning, or other private biography.

Do not lead with nationality, arrival in Germany, self-taught status, or dates
from Yaman's personal history. Humanity comes from first-person opinions,
direct responsibility, and honest recommendations, not from asking the reader
to feel sorry for him.

Do not turn generic page copy into a contract. Reply-time guarantees, a fixed
number of feedback rounds, and a fixed post-launch period belong in the written
offer when agreed. The platform note describes only verified current facts: the
public site is self-built, not a site builder or bought template; future admin
capabilities are described as being developed, never as already operating.

---

### C16 — Contact, technical note, proof, and FAQ

`/contact` is primarily for project requests. A rough idea is enough; project
type, budget range, and desired readiness date are optional context, each with
a clear “not sure yet” choice. Direct email remains open for any other purpose,
including a possible collaboration. Default reply channel is email; phone is
available when the visitor prefers it. Do not add WhatsApp, reply-time promises,
or an offer of detailed free planning. An attachment is optional and exists
only for material that helps explain a request.

`/stack` is a short technical note, not a hiring page, CV, service page, or
architecture essay. It names only technology currently used in Yaman's work:
TypeScript, React, TanStack Start, Elysia, PostgreSQL, parameterised SQL, and
Docker. It states that the public site and its technical foundation are
self-built, while administration tools are still being expanded. Shopify stays
on the services page until there is a relevant project to show.

`/work` presents selected personal projects with their honest status. It never
labels them as client work or manufactures business results. Prime Estate stays
explicitly in progress; the current project facts remain the authority for what
is named as live or built.

`/faq` answers eight practical questions in three groups: before the start,
scope and price, and during/after the project. It informs rather than negotiates
a public contract: no fixed payment schedule, instalments, feedback-round
count, post-launch term, training promise, or detailed free discovery is
published. Technical foundations for speed, structure, mobile use, and
discoverability may be named; marketing, rankings, and revenue are not
promised.

The landing page may surface the same eight FAQ entries after the process and
before the final CTA in accessible accordions. This is a deliberate sixth
section because it answers real hesitation at the moment of contact. `/faq`
keeps the full answers visible; both locations use one source of truth.

---

## Working method for future content decisions

Ask the owner only about high-consequence matters of identity, audience,
boundaries, or truth. The copywriter/agent owns wording, hierarchy, translation
adaptation, and other small editorial decisions, then presents a concise
recommendation in Arabic. The owner’s instinct is evidence about the desired
relationship, not a demand to turn every preference into a rigid rule.
